# Pfaffian / Holographic Algorithms Literature Map

Date: 2026-05-29

Status: private literature map only.

## Why This Is Adjacent

Pfaffian and holographic algorithms are interesting to Monogate because they
show a recurring pattern: a problem that looks combinatorially large can
sometimes become tractable when represented in the right algebraic basis. That
is adjacent to EML Advantage Lab's central question:

Does EML ever provide a materially better basis for search, proof shape,
compression, or teaching?

This map does not claim the mechanisms are the same.

## What To Track

| Topic | Why It Matters | Monogate Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pfaffian orientations | Certain counting problems reduce to determinants/Pfaffians. | Useful analogy for hidden tractability, not an EML result. |
| Matchgates | Local constraints compose into globally tractable signatures. | Possible inspiration for operator-tree composition tests. |
| Holographic reductions | A basis change can transform apparent complexity. | Useful lens for EML basis-change experiments. |
| Quantum analogues | Some tensor-network and fermionic structures use related algebra. | Exploratory only; no physics claim. |

## Candidate Future Experiment

PF-M2 should not try to prove an EML/Pfaffian bridge. It should build a small
comparison table:

- a standard combinatorial expression
- a known Pfaffian/matchgate representation
- an EML-tree representation if one is natural

## Non-Claims

- No claim that Pfaffians or holographic algorithms power EML.
- No claim that EML discovers holographic reductions.
- No claim that this explains quantum computation or physics.
- No theorem or open-problem claim.
