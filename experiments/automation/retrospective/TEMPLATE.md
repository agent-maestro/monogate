# E1 retrospective backfill — audit template

## Read this before using the template

**The audit has not been performed, and was not performed by the AI that built this scaffold. That is
deliberate, and it is not modesty.**

Classifying fifteen weeks of interventions into `direction` / `correction` / `taste` / `mechanical`
requires knowing what the human was thinking at the time — whether they redirected the work because
they smelled something wrong (`taste`) or because they had spotted an actual error (`correction`).
That distinction is the measurement E1 turns on, and it is not recoverable from a transcript by
anyone who was not there.

Worse, **an AI retroactively grading whether a human or an AI caught each error is a conflicted
instrument.** The system doing the classifying is the system whose contribution is being measured. Its
errors would fall in a predictable direction, it has no way to detect that they had, and every
classification would still look reasonable. This is the same failure this project has recorded
elsewhere as *self-authored traps* and *the instrument grading its own exam* — here it would be
running on the primary dataset of the study.

So: this template is scaffolding. The classification is orchestrator judgement, and the audit's value
depends on it staying that way.

## THE AUDIT'S PRIMARY JOB, promoted 2026-07-31: it is the CALIBRATION INSTRUMENT

**This was framed as a backfill. It is now the correction factor for E1's headline number, and that is
its main purpose.**

The live ledger reports a by-actor mix in which human interventions are overwhelmingly `direction`.
**Two hypotheses are observationally equivalent in that data:** the central claim is confirmed, or the
AI logs its own catches generously and compresses terse human redirections into `direction` by default.
**The confound is in the labelling function, not the sample size** — more sessions from the same
classifier converge on the same biased ratio with tighter error bars, which is a precise wrong number.

**The overlap set is what fixes it.** Where this audit and the live ledger label the *same* event, the
pair yields an **inter-rater agreement figure**, and that figure is the correction the ratio needs
before it means anything.

So, two additions to the per-session record below:

* **`ledger_entry:`** — when an audited intervention corresponds to a live ledger entry, record
  `<session-id>[<index>]`. These are the overlap set; without the pointer the pair is unrecoverable.
* **`agrees_with_ledger:`** `yes | no | n/a` — and where `no`, the disagreement is the datum, not an
  error to reconcile. **Do not go back and change either label to match.**

**The disagreement rate is plausibly the most honest number this experiment produces**, because *"how
differently do the human and the AI perceive who did what"* is a sharper question than the ratio it
corrupts.

A faster, forward-looking companion exists — `ledger.py dual`, AMENDMENT 6 — which labels new sessions
as they close. **It cannot cover the historical record. This audit is the only instrument that can**,
and that is why its priority changed.

## Per-session audit record

Copy one block per session audited. Append to `retrospective/records/`.

```
session:        <id or date + repo>
source:         <path to the log/commit this is drawn from>
auditor:        <who classified — a human>
audited_on:     <date>

interventions:
  - kind:        direction | correction | taste | mechanical
    boundary:    yes | no        # `?` flag — a judgement call, kept and counted separately
    ledger_entry: <session-id>[<index>] | none   # THE OVERLAP SET — see the calibration section
    agrees_with_ledger: yes | no | n/a           # where `no`, that IS the datum; change neither label
    evidence:    <quote or line reference from the source>
    note:        <why this kind and not the adjacent one>

findings:
  - class:       closure | surprise | falsification | dead_end
    artifact:    <link — house rule 6, no claim without an artifact>
    evidence:    <quote or line reference>

confidence:     high | medium | low
unrecoverable:  <what could not be classified from the record at all>
```

## Rules for the auditor

1. **`correction` requires an error to have existed.** "This is fine but boring" is `taste`. If you
   cannot establish that something was actually wrong, it is not a `correction`.
2. **Boundary cases are recorded with `boundary: yes`, never dropped.** A dropped ambiguous case is a
   silent thumb on the scale; a flagged one can be counted both ways later.
3. **`unrecoverable` is a real field and should be used.** A session whose interventions cannot be
   reconstructed is unmeasured, not zero. Backfilling a plausible guess to avoid a gap is the failure
   this whole program is built to detect.
4. **Do not classify a session you ran** if you can avoid it. Where unavoidable, record it — the
   conflict does not disappear by going unmentioned.
5. Retrospective records are **kept separate from live E1 ledger files** and reported separately. They
   are a different instrument with different error properties, and averaging them together would hide
   that.

## Known bias, stated in advance

The retrospective sample is drawn from what was written down. Sessions that produced nothing are the
least likely to have left a record, so the retrospective will over-represent productive sessions and
inflate findings-per-session relative to the live ledger. **This is the same blindness the live E1 gate
has** (it cannot see a session never logged), arriving through a different door. Expect the
retrospective and live numbers to disagree, and treat a disagreement as information about the
instruments rather than about the work.
