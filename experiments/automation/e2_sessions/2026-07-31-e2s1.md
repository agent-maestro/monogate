## Two disclosures that belong in the pre-registration, not in the write-up

**1. The selecting context is warm.** This target was chosen by an AI that had just spent six sessions
on EML asymptotics and had `EMLTreeValid.divisor_pos`, `inner_arms_coupled`, the totalization finding
and the `K/x` floor in working memory. That is not a violation — it is what an AI-selected target
*looks like*, and E2 exists to measure it. But if the `E2-ai` arm outperforms, "the AI picks targets
adjacent to what it just learned" is a live alternative explanation to "the AI selects well", and the
scoring must not get to choose between them silently.

**2. This is deliberately NOT the E5 target.** The E5 arm closed today at 6 of 6 with an explicit
"no further sessions without a fresh pre-registration and a fresh budget". Running `1/x ∉ EML` under an
E2 banner would be that arm spending a budget it was just denied, with the label changed. `x + 1 ∉ EML`
is a different open problem in the same object, with its own prior work and its own frontier note.

## Why this target and not another

Three candidates were considered from `grep`-level survey of the machlib frontier notes: the constructive
chain-length-2 bound over `IterExp 2` (`ChainExp2Instance.lean`, blocked on framework redesign — too
large for one session), the propagating-regularity circularity in `EMLPfaffian.lean` (same problem,
flagged as open in two separate files), and this one. This one was chosen because **it has a cheap
adversarial pass**: the E5 arm's repeated result is that trying to *build* the object first is the
highest-yield opening move, and `x+1` at depth 2 is small enough to attempt directly.

## Scoring notes — the parts that count against the arm

**The prediction was wrong in the direction that flatters the session, and that is worth stating
plainly.** Predicting `closure` and getting `falsification` is not a near miss; it is the difference
between "I expect to tidy this up" and "the claim is false". The mechanism was wrong too: the predicted
obstruction (`x+1` needed in the divisor position) does not exist, because the divisor position does
something the prediction had not noticed it could do.

**What the prediction got right, stated narrowly so it is not inflated:** that the 24 subcases would
collapse rather than need enumeration, and that the collapse would run through the divisor position.
Both true. The conclusion drawn from them was the opposite of the truth.

**For E2's measurement.** This is one AI-selected session producing two findings, one of them a
surprise — which runs *against* the pre-registered prediction that AI-selected sessions produce
closures and few surprises. n=1. The warm-context caveat filed in the PRE-SESSION block above applies
in full and applies *here specifically*: the negation gadget is `log ∘ exp = id` used in the divisor
position, which is the same identity the E5 arm had just been manipulating for six sessions. **A
cold-context AI, or a human, might not have reached for it — and equally, this session's success may be
measuring E5's residue rather than the AI's selection.** The scoring cannot distinguish those at n=1
and must not pretend to.

**One thing the session did not do.** It did not check whether the depth-4 witness is minimal. Depth 2
and 3 are unexamined for `x + 1`; the file's earlier work only closes depth ≤ 1. Recorded as an
unexamined gap rather than left to look like a settled bound.
